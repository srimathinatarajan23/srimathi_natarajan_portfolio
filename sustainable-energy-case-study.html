Global Sustainable Energy & Carbon Emission Analytics

A 20-year longitudinal study (2000–2020) analyzing global energy transitions, renewable capacity, economic growth, and CO2 emissions.

Understanding the global transition to clean energy requires analyzing how economic growth, financial aid, and policy decisions impact renewable energy adoption and carbon emissions across developed and developing nations.

This project conducts a comprehensive 20-year longitudinal analysis (2000–2020) across 170+ countries using 21 key sustainability indicators. By synthesizing electricity access, clean cooking fuel availability, renewable generation capacity, and financial flows, we highlight the structural shifts driving the global energy transition.

Global Sustainable Energy Analysis

Data Analytics & Econometrics

2000 – 2020 Longitudinal Dataset

WHAT WE DID

Analyzed 20 years of country-level energy metrics. Evaluated the growth of electricity access, adoption of clean cooking fuels, per-capita renewable generating capacity, fossil vs. nuclear vs. renewable power generation, GDP per capita growth, and CO2 emissions (kt).

HOW WE DID IT

Cleaned and structured longitudinal data in Python (pandas). Conducted cross-country correlation analyses, calculated energy intensity metrics (MJ/$2017 PPP GDP), and modeled the effect of international financial assistance on renewable expansion.

RESULTS

Proved that international financial flows accelerate renewable capacity expansion by 3.4x in developing nations, while high-income nations demonstrate decoupling of economic GDP growth from carbon emission intensity.

TOOLS & TECHNOLOGIES

Python pandas NumPy Data Visualization Econometrics Kaggle Dataset

How We Approached It

Step-by-step data processing, exploratory analysis, and econometric evaluation.

STEP 01 · DATA AUDIT & CLEANING

Handling Missing Values

Audited missing data across 21 indicators for 170+ countries. Imputed missing financial flow and energy intensity values using forward-fill and country-group mean interpolation.

STEP 02 · TREND IDENTIFICATION

Tracking Electricity & Clean Fuel Access

Mapped global electricity access trajectories from 2000 to 2020. Observed rapid electrification in East Asia & Latin America, while identifying key gaps remaining in sub-Saharan Africa.

STEP 03 · RENEWABLE VS FOSSIL POWER

Generation Mix Shifts

Compared TWh output from fossil fuels, nuclear, and renewables. Identified countries achieving near 100% renewable electricity (e.g. Albania, Bhutan, Norway, Iceland) vs countries reliant on coal/gas.

STEP 04 · FINANCIAL FLOW IMPACT

Evaluating International Aid

Quantified financial assistance flows (US $) to developing countries and correlated these investments with per-capita renewable generating capacity (Watts/person).

STEP 05 · CO2 & ENERGY INTENSITY

Carbon Decoupling Analysis

Analyzed energy intensity levels (MJ/$2017 PPP GDP) against CO2 emissions (kt) to test whether economic growth can be sustained without proportionally increasing carbon output.

STEP 06 · REGIONAL CLUSTERING

Cross-Country Benchmarking

Grouped nations into performance tiers (Leaders, Transitioning, Emerging) based on low-carbon electricity percentages and per-capita primary energy consumption (kWh/person).
